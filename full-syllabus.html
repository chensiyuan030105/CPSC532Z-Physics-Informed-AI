Course Description

This course explores the integration of modern machine learning techniques with physics-based modeling and simulation. Structured around foundational principles and emerging research, we will examine differentiable simulations, physics-informed learning, reduced-order modeling, and generative AI methods for physical systems. Topics span from graph neural networks and neural operators to diffusion models and AI-driven scientific discovery. Students will engage with key readings and hands-on projects, culminating in the creation of a differentiable simulator for a chosen application.

Seminar Structure

This research seminar uses a role-playing format. Students rotate through paper reviewer, archaeologist, industrial R&D expert, and hacker roles. Presentation assignments and submission details are TBD.

Course Goals

  • Develop differentiable simulations that combine physical principles and data-driven methods.
  • Compare physics-based and data-driven modeling approaches.
  • Apply graph neural networks, neural operators, and transformers to scientific problems.
  • Design, manage, and communicate an open-ended research project.

Assessment

Role play: 60% — 10 presentations, 6% each.

Final project: 40% — proposal 5%, mid-term check-in I 5%, mid-term check-in II 5%, final presentation 25%.

Final Project

Option 1: Implement and refine a differentiable simulation paper.

Option 2: Incorporate differentiable simulation into your own research.

Group size: 2–3 people.
